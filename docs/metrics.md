# How RiverCity Pulse measures

This page defines the numbers the API and dashboard show, says what they leave out and why, describes the clear-outs they count but annotate, and gives the SQL to recompute all of it. Exclusions are published with their counts at [`/api/meta/exclusions`](../README.md#api), and clear-out notes at [`/api/meta/clear-outs`](../README.md#api).

## Principles

- **Source records are never changed or deleted.** The cleaned table keeps every request as the city published it. Classifications and labels are computed next to the data and recomputed on every refresh.
- **Headline numbers follow a published definition.** That definition is this page.
- **An exclusion has to prove itself.** A record is left out only when its time to close is invalid (date problems) or it is not a service request (non-service). Real requests are never left out because they make a figure look worse. Overstating a delay is a defensible error; understating it isn't.
- **Anything left out stays visible**, as its own number with its reason, in `/api/meta/exclusions`.
- **Requests are classified by what they are, never by how fast they closed.** Dropping fast or slow closures because of their speed would be cherry-picking. Speed is only used to *find* candidates. The decision is made per request type, by hand, and recorded in a seed file with a reason.
- **Unusual events get notes, not removal.** A note is generated by a published rule, never picked by hand. It states facts and never says to ignore a number. It is shown where the number is. The same approach applies to events that flatter the city as to ones that don't (see [Symmetry check](#symmetry-check)).

## The headline numbers

| Figure | Definition |
|---|---|
| Median and p90 days to close | `PERCENTILE_CONT` over service requests closed in the period with a trustworthy time to close (no date problem). Clear-outs are included as recorded |
| Closed | How many requests the median and p90 are over |
| Excluded | Closed service requests left out of timing because of a date problem |
| Bulk closed | The part of Closed that was closed in a clear-out (for information; see [Clear-outs](#clear-outs-counted-and-noted)) |
| Opened | Service requests created in the period |
| Open now, median open age | Service requests open at the as-of time, and the median of their ages |
| Backlog series | Service requests opened, closed and open per day from 2024-01-01 |

A window of N days (30, 90 or 365) has a *current* period (the last N days through the as-of date, Sacramento time) and a *prior* period (the N days before it). The trend compares the two medians. It is null when either period has fewer than 30 closed requests, and "steady" within 5%.

## What is left out

### 1. Non-service requests (out of every figure)

Some records in the 311 feed are not requests for service: information calls answered on the phone, items waiting in an email inbox, referrals to someone else. They close in minutes or never, so counting them as service work pulls the median down (information calls) and inflates the open backlog (the inbox). They are left out of **every** headline figure, including opened, open now and the backlog series, and the categories list doesn't offer them as a filter. They are reported separately, with counts, in `/api/meta/exclusions`.

A request is non-service when either of these holds:

- **Its whole category is non-service** (`ref.category_map.is_service = 0`, with a reason). These are the Other and Process/Unclassified groups:

  | CategoryLevel1 | Reason |
  |---|---|
  | Other | Information and referral calls (mostly "Information" and "Non City"), logged and closed during the call |
  | Review | An inbox bucket (mostly "Email Review"): items waiting to be read, not requests for service |
  | Escalation | A routing bucket (to a specialist or supervisor): says how a call was handled, not what service was asked for |
  | Web Form | A form bucket (mostly "Mayor Form"): correspondence, not a request for service |
  | Community Tag, Homeless Services, DCR, CDD-Planning | Too few requests to report on their own, so grouped as Other |

- **Its line inside a service category is non-service** (`ref.non_service_type`, a CategoryLevel1 + CategoryLevel2 pair with a reason):

  | Category / line | Reason |
  |---|---|
  | Animal Control / General - Other | Information call on another topic |
  | Animal Control / General - Duplicate Request | A duplicate of a request already logged; the original is counted |
  | Animal Control / General - After Hours Call | An after-hours call logged for the record |
  | Animal Control / General - Adoption, - Spay/ Neuter, - Investigation, - License, - Found Animal, - Barking | Information calls on those topics (the work itself has its own lines, e.g. "Found Animal", "Complaint - Barking") |
  | Building and Planning / General | Information call |
  | Parking / General | Information call |
  | Parks / General | Information call |
  | Urban Forestry / General | Information call |
  | Shared Rideables / Lime, Shared Rideables / Bird | Referred to the operator; the city does not do the work |

Categories that aren't in the map ("Unmapped") count as service, so nothing disappears silently. Those requests already carry the `UnmappedCategory` data-quality flag.

**How the lines were chosen (2026-10-04).** The candidate query below lists every category line with 100+ requests since 2024 where 90% or more of the closures happened within an hour. Each candidate was then judged by its name. Lines that are information, duplicates, after-hours logging or referrals were added. Lines that name real work stay counted, however fast they close:

| Candidate (closed within an hour) | Decision |
|---|---|
| Code Enforcement / Business Compliance Taxi Cab (96%) | Counted: a compliance case |
| Homeless Camp / Homeless Camp-Trash SPD (99%) | Counted: a cleanup |
| Parks / Request Ranger (90%) | Counted: a ranger dispatch |
| Streets / Alley Gate (96%) | Counted: names a work item |

Lines under 90% are not candidates, so they stay counted even when the name says "General": Animal Control / General (89%), Streets / General (88%), Facilities / General (81%), Code Enforcement / General (74% in the last 90 days) and Homeless Camp / General (72%). Solid Waste, Water and Utility Billing "General" are real work (0–0.2% within an hour).

To change the list, edit `db/seed/category_map.sql` or `db/seed/non_service_type.sql` and run `worker migrate`. The next refresh reclassifies every request, with no reclean needed.

### 2. Date problems (out of the median and p90 only)

Closed requests whose time to close can't be trusted:

| Flag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `SentinelDate` | A date before 2000 (the 1899-12-30 field default) was set to NULL |
| `FutureDate` | A date more than one day after the time it was cleaned |
| `InvalidCloseOrder` | DateClosed is earlier than DateCreated |
| `ClosedMissingDate` | The status is CLOSED but DateClosed is empty |

They still count as closed for the backlog. `/api/meta/exclusions` lists each flag with its count; a request can have two.

## Clear-outs (counted and noted)

Sometimes a department closes a large batch of old requests at once. On 2026-08-10, Parking closed 10,820 requests averaging 564 days old in one minute. These closures are **counted as recorded**: those requests really did sit open that long in the city's own records, and no count of closures proves that a clear-out wasn't real work. Instead, each clear-out gets a **note**, a one-sentence fact shown next to the figures it affects: on the backlog chart at its date, in the hover card of the category or neighborhood, and in the "How we measure" panel.

**Rule** (`Sac311.Domain.BulkClosureRule`, applied by `usp_classify_for_metrics` as the `BulkClosure` label):

- **Day clause:** a (closed date, category group) with at least **100** closures older than **180 days**.
- **Sweep clause:** one closed minute (UTC) with at least **50** closures older than **180 days**, across any categories. This catches cross-department sweeps such as 2026-08-21 08:00 UTC (Water, Sewer, Drains, Solid Waste) and 2026-09-03 06:53 UTC (Utility Billing, Solid Waste, Water, Sewer), which the day clause only partly caught.
- **Members:** every closure in a clear-out day or sweep minute that is older than **90 days** (the August 10 minute also closed 576 Parking requests aged 90–180 days). Only service requests with no date problem count.
- The values live in code and on this page; there is no configuration setting.

The label can't change any figure, so a broader rule costs nothing in honesty; it only decides which events get a note. `bulkClosed` says how many of a period's closed requests were in a clear-out. The data-quality checks count `BulkClosure` labels after every run, so a new clear-out raises a warning.

**Notes** (`GET /api/meta/clear-outs?from=&to=&category=`): one row per clear-out day and category since 2024-01-01, with the count, the average age, the minutes from first to last closure, whether it was part of a sweep and which other categories closed old requests in that minute, plus the generated sentence. On 2026-10-04 there were 29 rows (16,363 requests): 8 in 2025 and 21 since 2026-08-10, 12 of them part of a sweep. Examples:

> On 2026-08-10, Parking closed 10,820 requests averaging 564 days old in one minute. In the same minute, Animal Control and Code Enforcement also closed old requests.
>
> On 2026-08-21, Water closed 223 requests averaging 554 days old in one minute. In the same minute, Sewer, Drains, Solid Waste and Animal Control also closed old requests.
>
> On 2026-10-03, Streets closed 621 requests averaging 701 days old over 135 minutes.

**Below the note threshold:** smaller batches get no note, and they are counted like everything else. Examples on 2026-10-04: Parking closed 99 requests older than 180 days on 2026-09-14, and 90 on each of 2026-08-26 and 2026-09-24, spread over many minutes. They are listed here as possible clear-outs, not as notes. The thresholds were set before looking at them and are not moved to catch them.

## Symmetry check

A note rule that only explained bad news would be one-sided, so the mirror case was looked for on 2026-10-04: a service category with a day (or a minute) of many closures within an hour of creation that isn't a known non-service line, such as a batch of same-minute create-and-close records. With the clear-out thresholds mirrored, the largest day had **85** such closures (Animal Control, 2025-05-28) against a threshold of 100, and the largest minute had **23** (Homeless Camp, 2025-07-27) against 50. **Checked, none found**, so there is no note kind for it. The busiest days are ordinary work: Animal Control "General" calls, and Drains "Rain Patrol" on storm days.

```sql
-- Same thresholds as the clear-out rule, mirrored: closures within an hour of creation instead of after 180 days.
WITH fast AS
(
    SELECT closed_date_local, category_group, DATEADD(minute, DATEDIFF(minute, 0, closed_utc), 0) AS closed_minute
    FROM dbo.service_request
    WHERE is_service = 1 AND source_removed_utc IS NULL AND dq_flags & 15 = 0 AND closed_date_local >= '2024-01-01'
      AND days_to_close <= 0.04
)
SELECT 'day' AS kind, MAX(n) AS largest FROM (SELECT COUNT(*) AS n FROM fast GROUP BY closed_date_local, category_group) AS d
UNION ALL
SELECT 'minute', MAX(n) FROM (SELECT COUNT(*) AS n FROM fast GROUP BY closed_minute) AS m;
```

## Effect (90-day window to 2026-10-04, citywide)

| | Closed requests timed | Median days | p90 days |
|---|---|---|---|
| Everything except date problems | 157,135 | 2.02 | 178.7 |
| **Headline: without non-service** | **122,273** | **5.89** | **328.0** |

Information calls close in minutes, so leaving them in would pull the median down to 2.02 days and flatter the city. Unread inbox items would nearly double the backlog:

- **Open now:** **45,795** service requests (87,789 with non-service: 41,994 non-service, 41,166 of them inbox items). Median open age **104** days (275 with non-service).
- **Trend:** the citywide median is **21.4% slower** than the prior 90 days (5.89 vs 4.85). The clear-outs since 2026-08-10 are most of the change, and their notes say so.
- **Long tails are visible:** Parking's median is 532.6 days and its p90 872.9 (13,358 of its 14,880 closures were in clear-outs); p90s are 455.0 for Streets, 280.8 for Sewer and 238.3 for Drains. Neighborhoods with 30+ closed range from 1.01 to 704.68 days (Pell/Main Industrial Park 704.68, Old Sacramento 473.89).

## Method change, 2026-10-04

From Session 7b (earlier on 2026-10-04) until this change, bulk closures were left out of the median and p90 (`is_metric_eligible` was `dq_flags & 4111`). They are now counted as recorded and described by notes, for the reasons in [Principles](#principles): no count rule proves a clear-out wasn't real work, the 100-closure cutoff treated identical Parking batches of 99 and 100 differently, and every way of leaving them out made the city look faster. The non-service exclusion stays. The change applies to all periods; nothing is computed under the old method any more.

90-day window to 2026-10-04, same data, before and after:

| | Before (bulk closures out) | After (counted) |
|---|---|---|
| Citywide closed / median / p90 | 107,909 / 3.88 / 37.6 | 122,273 / 5.89 / 328.0 |
| Citywide trend vs prior 90 days | 20% faster | 21.4% slower |
| `excluded` | 22,413 (14,364 bulk + 8,049 date problems) | 8,049 (date problems) |
| Parking median / p90 | 2.91 / 626.6 | 532.6 / 872.9 |
| Streets p90 | 88.0 | 455.0 |
| Water p90 | 33.0 | 51.9 |
| Old Sacramento median / p90 | 10.15 / 608.3 | 473.89 / 792.2 |
| Pell/Main Industrial Park median / p90 | 0.93 / 73.4 | 704.68 / 810.8 |
| Neighborhood medians (30+ closed) | 0.93 to 14.24 | 1.01 to 704.68 |
| Open now, median open age | 45,795, 104 days | unchanged |

The Session 7b commits stay in the history. Migration 0012 set the mask back to `dq_flags & 15`, and clear-outs moved from `/api/meta/exclusions` to `/api/meta/clear-outs`.

## Check it yourself

Recompute the 90-day citywide median and p90 from the cleaned table and the seeds alone, without the stored classification. This gives the same numbers as `/api/categories/summary?window=90` for the same as-of date. `dq_flags & 15 = 0` keeps requests with none of the four date-problem flags, and `dbo.fn_map_key` is the SQL copy of the key function the seeds use.

```sql
DECLARE @as_of date = '2026-10-04', @window int = 90;
WITH typed AS
(
    SELECT r.*, dbo.fn_map_key(r.category_level1) AS level1_key, dbo.fn_map_key(r.category_level2) AS level2_key
    FROM dbo.service_request AS r
    WHERE r.source_removed_utc IS NULL
),
svc AS
(
    SELECT t.* FROM typed AS t
    WHERE NOT EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM ref.category_map AS cm WHERE cm.category_key = t.level1_key AND cm.is_service = 0)
      AND NOT EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM ref.non_service_type AS n WHERE n.category_key = t.level1_key AND n.category_level2_key = t.level2_key)
)
SELECT DISTINCT
    COUNT(*) OVER () AS closed,
    PERCENTILE_CONT(.5) WITHIN GROUP (ORDER BY days_to_close) OVER () AS median_days,
    PERCENTILE_CONT(.9) WITHIN GROUP (ORDER BY days_to_close) OVER () AS p90_days
FROM svc
WHERE dq_flags & 15 = 0 AND days_to_close IS NOT NULL
  AND closed_date_local > DATEADD(day, -@window, @as_of) AND closed_date_local <= @as_of;
```

On 2026-10-04 this returned 122,273 closed, median 5.89 and p90 327.98, the same as the API.

The clear-outs, from the rule alone. `is_service` is the stored non-service classification (the query above shows how it is derived). On 2026-10-04 this returned the same 29 rows as `agg.clear_out`.

```sql
DECLARE @min_count int = 100, @sweep_min_count int = 50, @detect_age_days int = 180, @member_age_days int = 90;
WITH svc AS
(
    SELECT closed_date_local, category_group, days_to_close, DATEADD(minute, DATEDIFF(minute, 0, closed_utc), 0) AS closed_minute
    FROM dbo.service_request
    WHERE is_service = 1 AND source_removed_utc IS NULL AND dq_flags & 15 = 0 AND closed_utc IS NOT NULL AND closed_date_local >= '2024-01-01'
),
clear_out_day AS
(
    SELECT closed_date_local, category_group FROM svc WHERE days_to_close > @detect_age_days
    GROUP BY closed_date_local, category_group HAVING COUNT(*) >= @min_count
),
sweep_minute AS
(
    SELECT closed_minute FROM svc WHERE days_to_close > @detect_age_days
    GROUP BY closed_minute HAVING COUNT(*) >= @sweep_min_count
)
SELECT s.closed_date_local AS day, s.category_group, COUNT(*) AS closed, CAST(AVG(s.days_to_close) AS decimal(9, 2)) AS avg_age_days
FROM svc AS s
WHERE s.days_to_close > @member_age_days
  AND (EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM clear_out_day AS d WHERE d.closed_date_local = s.closed_date_local AND d.category_group = s.category_group)
       OR EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM sweep_minute AS m WHERE m.closed_minute = s.closed_minute))
GROUP BY s.closed_date_local, s.category_group
ORDER BY day, category_group;
```

The candidate query used to find non-service lines (it finds candidates only, and each one is then judged by its name):

```sql
SELECT category_group, category_level1, category_level2, COUNT(*) AS requests,
       CAST(100.0 * SUM(CASE WHEN days_to_close <= 0.04 THEN 1 ELSE 0 END)
            / NULLIF(SUM(CASE WHEN days_to_close IS NOT NULL THEN 1 ELSE 0 END), 0) AS decimal(5, 1)) AS pct_closed_within_hour
FROM dbo.service_request
WHERE created_date_local >= '2024-01-01' AND category_group NOT IN ('Other', 'Process/Unclassified')
GROUP BY category_group, category_level1, category_level2
HAVING COUNT(*) >= 100
   AND 100.0 * SUM(CASE WHEN days_to_close <= 0.04 THEN 1 ELSE 0 END)
       / NULLIF(SUM(CASE WHEN days_to_close IS NOT NULL THEN 1 ELSE 0 END), 0) >= 90
ORDER BY category_group, requests DESC;
```

`days_to_close` is stored to two decimals, so `<= 0.04` means within an hour.

## Checked and left alone

- **Solid Waste's nightly batch closes:** about 290 closures at around 6:01 AM Pacific, averaging about 30 days old. That is real bulky-pickup work recorded by a nightly sync, and too young to be a clear-out.
- **Utility Billing / General:** median 1.00 and p90 1.01 days. It looks like a fixed one-day process.
- **Old open requests** in Streets (back to 2022) and Facilities (765 over 2 years) are a real backlog.
- **Long p90 tails** (Parking, Streets, Sewer, Drains) are real tails. The p90 is there to show them, and the clear-out notes explain the largest.
- **A repeat address** (one address with 100+ new requests in 7 days, e.g. 6005 Wardell Way's web "Owned Animal Complaint" requests) is a data-quality warning, not an exclusion.
